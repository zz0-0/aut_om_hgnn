## Summary of major changes

1. **Broadened state-estimation scope.** The framework now estimates seven
   quantities instead of three: per-foot contact, per-foot 3-D GRF, base twist
   (linear + angular velocity), total ground reaction force, base angular
   acceleration, joint acceleration, and joint friction torque. All targets are
   expressed in the base frame, and the multi-task variant predicts all seven
   with a single forward/backward pass.
2. **New data generation with physically meaningful labels.** The simulator
   export now logs body-frame kinematics/forces and an explicitly injected
   Coulomb + viscous joint-friction torque, so friction and angular
   acceleration have exact, verifiable labels (no finite-difference proxies
   required).
3. **Controlled evaluation protocol.** Models are trained on locomotion data
   generated by our own controlled policy and evaluated on a held-out test
   split of whole trajectories. We make no cross-robot transfer claims; all
   comparisons are within-robot.
4. **Method section expanded** with formal definitions of the graph, features
   and units, temporal window encoding, message passing, symmetry action,
   losses, and computational cost. The equivariance statement was narrowed to
   what is actually guaranteed by the architecture, with a numerical
   consistency check reported instead of an unsupported claim.
5. **Data splitting corrected.** Train/validation/test splits are now made at
   the **trajectory (episode) level**, eliminating leakage from overlapping
   history windows. All reported results use the corrected split.
6. **Labeling and units corrected**, including renaming the misleading "CoM"
   head to **base velocity** and fixing the pressure/dimensionless unit table.
7. **Paper reorganized** around three research questions, with per-foot and
   per-axis tables moved to the appendix and causal language corrected.

## Response to Comment 1

> _You may consider broadening the current scope to a wider range of
> proprioceptive state estimation, such as joint friction, body angular
> acceleration, and ground total reaction force, etc._

We thank the reviewer for this suggestion and have substantially broadened the
estimation scope. Note first that **per-foot 3-D GRF estimation already existed**
in the original submission (three force components per foot, all feet predicted
jointly); we now state this explicitly early in the results.

The revision adds the following targets (the three requested plus two natural
extensions):

| Target | Symbol | Node | Channels | Frame | Loss | Metrics |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Contact state | $c$ | foot | 1 / foot | – | BCE | Acc., P, R, F1, Spec., MCC (+ per-foot) |
| Per-foot GRF | $\mathbf{F}_i$ | foot | 3 / foot | base | MSE | MAE/MSE/RMSE/R² overall, per-axis, per-foot, mass/area-normalized |
| Base velocity | $\mathbf{v}_b,\boldsymbol{\omega}_b$ | base | 6 | base | MSE | MAE/MSE/RMSE/R² + cosine, overall and linear/angular |
| **Total GRF** | $\mathbf{F}_{\text{tot}}$ | base | 3 | base | MSE | overall + per-axis + weight-normalized |
| **Base angular acceleration** | $\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_b$ | base | 3 | base | MSE | overall + per-axis |
| **Joint acceleration** | $\ddot{q}_j$ | joint | 1 / joint | – | MSE | overall |
| **Joint friction** | $\tau_{f,j}$ | joint | 1 / joint | – | MSE | overall |

**Total ground reaction force.** We report total GRF in two complementary ways,
as recommended: (i) as a directly predicted quantity attached to the base node,
and (ii) as a **derived evaluation** obtained by summing the per-foot
predictions,
$$\hat{\mathbf{F}}_{\text{tot}}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{N_f}\hat{\mathbf{F}}_i(t),$$
with RMSE/MAE/R² per axis and predicted-vs-true time series during walking. We
present the derived variant as an additional evaluation of the existing GRF
head rather than as a separate modeling contribution.

**Body angular acceleration.** Estimated at the base node. Labels are taken
from the IMU's body-frame angular acceleration (computed at physics rate and
sampled at the 50 Hz control rate), so no additional filtering assumptions are
required; the label source, frame, and sampling rate are documented in the
method.

**Joint friction.** We enable joint friction in the simulator as an explicit
Coulomb + viscous torque injected at each actuator,
$$\tau_{f,j}= -\big(\mu_j\,\mathrm{sign}(\dot q_j) + c_{v,j}\,\dot q_j\big),$$
with per-environment randomized coefficients $(\mu_j, c_{v,j})$ sampled from
configured ranges. The injected torque is logged exactly, which gives a
verifiable label and avoids conflating unmodeled solver effects with friction.
We describe this as an extension of the estimation benchmark, with its friction
model stated explicitly; broader friction identification (e.g., load-dependent
models) is left to future work.

The dataset exporter now records 25 per-step fields, including
`root_com_{lin,ang}_vel_b`, `foot/hand_{pos,lin_vel}_b`,
`contact_forces_b`, `total_grf_b`, `imu_ang_acc_b`, `joint_acc`, and
`joint_friction`, plus `control_dt` metadata.

## Response to Comment 2

> _You may consider integrating some basic locomotion control tasks into your
> experiment session to help validate the accuracy, estimation latency, and
> hardware feasibility of your framework on some real robots._

We addressed this along three axes, and we are explicit about the evidence
boundary for each.

### (a) Locomotion is present; we now describe it precisely

The datasets are generated by rolling out a velocity-tracking locomotion
controller (PPO) in IsaacLab on rough terrain, with randomized commands
(forward speed, yaw rate), pushes, and terrain curriculum. The estimator is
trained and evaluated on proprioception recorded during these locomotion
rollouts (start/stop, contact transitions, turning). This was true in the
original submission but under-described; the revised experimental setup
specifies the terrain, command distribution, control rate (50 Hz), episode
length, and the number of environments/episodes.

### (b) Scope of the controller validation

We considered adding a zero-shot test on data generated by an independently
released controller, but the released policies available to us cannot be
operated under the data-generation protocol: datasets are generated with
identical simulator settings (robot model, terrain, command distribution,
friction injection, domain randomization, environment/episode/step counts, and
exporter fields), and the released humanoid policy fails under those
conditions. In a controlled sandbox study the policy walks in its native
environment but falls within ~1.5 s in ours, even after matching its
observation/action interface and restricting commands to its training range;
retraining it from the released configuration yields a standing controller.
We therefore restrict the revision to within-controller evaluation on held-out
trajectories and do not claim unseen-controller robustness. This is stated
explicitly as an evidence boundary in the paper.

### (c) Latency and online operation

We report estimation latency following the recommended protocol:

- deployment target: batch size **1**; measured on GPU and CPU;
- components reported separately: proprioceptive preprocessing, graph/window
  construction, and network forward pass;
- **mean and P95** latency, effective inference frequency, and memory;
- comparison of **H = 1 vs. H = 150** history; H = 150 corresponds to
  **3.0 s** at 50 Hz. We clarify that the window is applied causally, that the
  buffer is filled at startup by repeating the first frame, and we quantify the
  output lag this introduces.
- A streaming estimator with an internal history buffer is provided and is
  numerically identical to the offline pipeline.

We additionally run a **sim-online** evaluation in which the estimator consumes
the same 50 Hz proprioceptive stream as the locomotion controller and is
compared against simulator ground truth over full episodes. Concretely, the
locomotion policy and the streaming estimator run concurrently; at every 20 ms
control step the estimator receives the current proprioception, updates its
causal history buffer, and produces estimates for all heads. We report:

- batch-1 inference latency (feature preparation, history update, graph
  construction, forward pass) with mean/p50/p95/max, and the number of control
  deadlines missed (latency > 20 ms);
- online MAE/RMSE per head against simulator ground truth over 1,500 control
  steps (30 s), for the internal controller;
- the same comparison for H = 1 and H = 150 to quantify the accuracy/latency
  trade-off, including the startup buffer-fill behavior (the history is padded
  by repeating the first frame) and the resulting output lag.

Representative measured numbers (single RTX 5080, batch 1, H = 150): inference
1.4–3.2 ms mean, P95 ≤ 3.9 ms, comfortably below the 20 ms control budget with
no deadline misses.

### (d) Hardware: what we can and cannot claim

We do not have a reliable independent reference for real GRF or base velocity
(mocap or foot-force sensing) on the physical robots, and deploying a robust
locomotion controller on hardware is outside the scope of this revision.
Accordingly, the revised paper **does not claim validated accuracy on real
robots**. Instead:

- we report sim-online accuracy, latency, and computational feasibility;
- we provide a deployment-ready streaming interface and document the
  measurement protocol required for hardware accuracy (independent references);
- real-robot validation is stated as the immediate next step in the discussion,
  rather than implied by the simulation results.

## Response to Comment 3

> _The theoretical part can be expanded to explicitly analyze and demonstrate
> the technical details of your framework._

Section 3 has been rewritten from prose into explicit definitions.

**Graph.** A heterogeneous graph $\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})$ is
parsed from the robot USD. Biped: base, joint, foot, hand. Quadruped: base,
joint, foot. The kinematic tree defines typed, directed edge pairs
(base↔joint, joint↔joint, joint↔foot, joint↔hand), traversed in both
directions for message passing. Counts per robot are reported explicitly
(e.g., G1: 1 base, 24 joints, 2 feet, 2 hands; edges 3 base–joint, 21
joint–joint, 2 joint–foot, 2 joint–hand, each bidirectional).

**Features and units.** Per node type and per time step: base — IMU linear
acceleration and angular velocity (3 + 3, body frame, m/s², rad/s); joint —
position, velocity, torque (1 + 1 + 1, rad, rad/s, N·m); foot — base-frame
position and linear velocity relative to the base (3 + 3, m, m/s); hand — same
as foot. Feature types (polar vector / axial vector / scalar) are declared per
channel block and drive the symmetry action.

**Temporal encoding.** A history of $H$ control steps is flattened in
time-major order into a single channel vector per node,
$\mathbf{x}_i=[\mathbf{x}_i^{(t-H+1)},\dots,\mathbf{x}_i^{(t)}]$, and labels
correspond to the last step. The window is strictly causal. $H=150$
corresponds to 3.0 s at 50 Hz.

**Message passing.** For layer $l$,
$$\mathbf{h}_i^{(l+1)}=\sigma\Big(\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_e(i)}W_{e}\,\mathbf{h}_j^{(l)}\Big),$$
with one shared weight matrix per edge type and sum aggregation; the MS-HGNN
variant additionally applies symmetry-edge convolutions with mean aggregation
and residual connections.

**Symmetry action.** For the biped the group is $C_2$ (sagittal reflection)
and for the quadruped $K_4\cong\mathbb{Z}_2\times\mathbb{Z}_2$
(front–back, left–right, and their composition). The morphology defines a
node permutation $\pi_g$ ($\pi_g\circ\pi_h=\pi_{gh}$) and a per-block sign
action $\rho_g$: polar vectors transform as $S_g\mathbf{v}$ and axial vectors
as $\det(S_g)S_g\boldsymbol{\omega}$, with
$S_g=\mathrm{diag}(\pm 1,\pm 1,\pm 1)$; scalars are invariant. Because all
geometric features and all regression targets are expressed in the base frame,
$\rho_g$ is exact for arbitrary base orientation. The adjacency is verified to
be invariant under $\pi_g$ (true kinematic edges), and labels are transformed
with the same action as the inputs. We also provide the measured deviation
$\lVert f(T_g x)-T_g f(x)\rVert$ for trained models in the appendix; we do not
claim strict equivariance that the dense encoder/decoder does not provide.

**Outputs and losses.** Regression targets use MSE; contact uses binary
cross-entropy. All heads, their node type, channel count, frame, loss, and
metric suite are listed in Table 1 (above).

**Compute.** Per-graph cost is
$O\big((|\mathcal{E}_{\text{kin}}|+|\mathcal{E}_{\text{sym}}|)d+|V|d^2\big)$
per layer; symmetry edges add $O(|V|)$ edges. Inference is a single forward
pass independent of episode length; measured latency is reported as above.

**Credit and novelty.** The backbone follows the original MI-HGNN / MS-HGNN
formulations; the revision explicitly states which components are inherited,
which are adapted to the USD/IsaacLab setting (kinematic-tree construction,
base-frame features, corrected symmetry permutations, label-consistent
symmetry transforms), and which are new in this work (broadened targets,
multi-task heads, friction injection, evaluation protocol).

**Model variants.** MI-HGNN and MS-HGNN are trained under identical settings
for each robot and history length; no other architectural ablations are
claimed in this revision.

## Response to the organizational comment

> _Overall, this topic is attractive; however, the logical organization of this
> paper is in need of great improvement._

The paper is restructured around three research questions:

1. **RQ1** — How accurately can the same HGNN architecture estimate the
   broadened set of proprioceptive states on each robot?
2. **RQ2** — Where does the symmetry mechanism help, and where does it not
   (including exceptions at H = 150 on the quadruped)?
3. **RQ3** — Does the history window justify its computational cost?

Correspondingly: the Introduction states the cross-morphology *evaluation*
framing and the three questions; the Method gives the full definitions above;
the Experimental Setup specifies splits, tasks, model variants, training, and
evaluation; the Results answer RQ1–RQ3 with time-series and
error analyses; the Discussion states applicability, failure cases, and
evidence boundaries; the Conclusion only summarizes what the experiments
support. Per-foot and per-axis tables are moved to the appendix, and the page
numbering/table references are fixed.

## Additional corrections raised in internal review

1. **"CoM" target.** The head previously named "center of mass" actually
   estimates **base linear and angular velocity**. It is now named
   `BASE_VELOCITY`, and the paper states the target definition, frame, and the
   relationship to the IMU input (in particular, on the quadruped the IMU is
   mounted on the base, so the angular component is close to the raw input;
   linear vs. angular results are reported separately and this is discussed).
2. **Data splits.** Earlier random window-level splits allowed overlapping
   H = 150 windows to cross the train/validation boundary. Splits are now made
   by whole `(environment, episode)` trajectories with a fixed split seed; all
   results were regenerated. A held-out test split of trajectories is also
   reported.
3. **Units.** Corrected table: $F/A$ is pressure (Pa), $F/(mg)$ is
   dimensionless, and $F/(mgA)$ has units m⁻². The mass-normalized metrics are
   no longer described as "acceleration units".
4. **Symmetry is not always beneficial.** The abstract and results now state
   the exceptions (e.g., quadruped H = 150 regression RMSE slightly worse for
   MS-HGNN) instead of claiming uniform improvement.
5. **Causal language.** Statements such as "G1's CoM motion is more regular"
   or "hand nodes improve message passing" are either supported by a new
   analysis/ablation or rephrased as hypotheses.

## New experiments and artifacts in the revision

- 7 estimation targets for 2 robots × 2 model variants × 2 history settings,
  run with 10 seeds; multi-task variants train all 7 heads jointly.
- Corrected trajectory-level splits; all baseline numbers regenerated.
- Derived total-GRF evaluation from per-foot predictions with per-axis metrics
  and time-series curves.
- Normalized multi-task objective (per-head loss divided by the target
  mean-square of the training data) so the optimization problem is identical
  across variants.
- Sim-online evaluation and latency benchmark (batch 1, mean/p50/p95, H = 1 vs.
  H = 150, startup-buffer behavior, deadline misses).
- Streaming estimator verified to match the offline pipeline numerically.

## Headline results (10 seeds, internal-controller protocol)

- Contact accuracy: 0.975–0.985 across all configurations.
- MS-HGNN matches or improves MI-HGNN on ground reaction force, total GRF,
  base angular acceleration, and joint acceleration in nearly all cells, with
  documented exceptions at H = 150 on the quadruped (base-velocity RMSE
  0.070 → 0.075, base angular acceleration 6.8 → 7.1, joint acceleration
  77 → 83).
- Sim-online validation: no control-deadline misses; batch-1 inference
  1.4–3.2 ms mean, P95 ≤ 3.9 ms at 50 Hz (20 ms budget), over 1,500 control
  steps against simulator ground truth.

## Evidence boundary

The revision supports: cross-morphology *evaluation* of a single HGNN method;
within-controller evaluation on held-out trajectories; and simulated
real-time feasibility. It does not claim inter-robot transfer, robustness to
unseen controllers, strict equivariance of the learned network, or validated
real-robot estimation accuracy; each of these is explicitly stated as a
boundary and/or future work.
